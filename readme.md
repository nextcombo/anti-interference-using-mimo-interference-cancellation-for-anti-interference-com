# [Anti-interference]Using MIMO Interference Cancellation for Anti-interference Communication[with MATLAB Code]

Interference attacks pose a serious threat to wireless communication. Reactive interference maximizes attack efficiency by interfering only when the target is communicating, which can be easily achieved with software-defined radios (SDR). This paper explores the use of multi-input multi-output (MIMO) technology for anti-interference orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) communication. Specifically, MIMO interference cancellation treats interference signals as noise and strategically eliminates them, while transmit precoding adjusts signal directions to optimize decoding performance. We first investigate the reactive interference strategy and its impact on MIMO-OFDM receivers. Then, we propose an anti-interference scheme based on MIMO that leverages MIMO interference cancellation and transmit precoding techniques to convert non-connected scenarios affected by interference into operable networks. We implemented the anti-interference communication scheme using SDR. The testbed evaluation demonstrates the destructive nature of reactive interference attacks while validating the effectiveness and efficiency of our defense mechanism in response to various types of interference signals with varying power levels from reactive interferers.
Introduction: Interference has always been a severe threat in wireless networks. Interferers intentionally emit interference signals to disrupt network communication, leading to reduced throughput, network partitioning, or complete disconnection. Reactive interference is one of the most effective forms of interference attacks. Reactive interferers continuously monitor channel activity, launching interference signals whenever they detect activity, otherwise remaining silent during the sender's idle time. Reactive interference is considered one of the most effective, stealthy, and energy-efficient strategies. The recent advancements in highly programmable SDR have made this complex yet powerful interference attack very realizable, proving that reactive interferers are easy to implement and result in destructive interference. Moreover, reactive interference can trigger quickly at any field of data packets, making it a realistic threat in wireless communication.
Modern broadband wireless communication systems, such as WLAN, digital television, and cellular communications, employ Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) as one of their core technologies. OFDM enhances the system's robustness against multipath fading and harsh noise environments, but it is not ideal in scenarios where adversaries attempt to intentionally interfere with the communication. This increasingly hostile environment with advanced interference threats has spurred the development of interference-resistant OFDM communication systems. Recent research investigates and attempts to mitigate the impact of interference attacks on OFDM systems, but they are specifically designed for opponents who know the pilot position and require very strict synchronization. Moreover, when all OFDM subcarriers (including the pilot) are interfered with in a reactive interference attack, their defense mechanisms cannot recover the signal.

Conclusion: Current wireless communication systems are highly susceptible to advanced interference attacks, especially powerful reactive interference attacks supported by software defined radio technology. Although there are no effective anti-interference solutions to protect OFDM communication, we utilize MIMO technology to defend against such attacks. We show that such attacks can severely disrupt MIMO-OFDM communication by controlling the interference signal vector in the antenna spatial domain. Therefore, we propose a defense mechanism based on interference cancellation and transmit precoding technology to maintain OFDM communication under reactive interference. Our prototype experimental results indicate that although MIMO-OFDM communication can be completely suppressed by interference attacks, our defense mechanism can effectively convert it into a running scenario with comparable throughput under different types of interference attacks.
